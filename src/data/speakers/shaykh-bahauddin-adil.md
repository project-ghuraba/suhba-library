Shaykh Bahā'uddīn ʿĀdil Efendi is the youngest son of Sulṭān al-Awliyā Shaykh Muḥammad Nāẓim ʿĀdil al-Ḥaqqānī and Ḥājjah Amīna ʿĀdil Sulṭān Ḥānim, and the younger brother of Mawlānā Shaykh Muḥammad ʿĀdil ar-Rabbānī, the current 41st Grand Shaykh of the Naqshbandi Ṭarīqah. Raised within one of the most influential spiritual households of the contemporary Muslim world, Shaykh Bahā'uddīn grew up immersed in the daily life, teachings, and service culture of the Naqshbandī-Ḥaqqānī tradition.

Based primarily in Lefke, he plays an important role in supporting and maintaining the family dergāh and the international community of murīds and visitors who travel from around the world to the Naqshbandī centre. For decades, the dergāh established by Mawlānā Shaykh Nāẓim has served as a spiritual home and gathering place for seekers from diverse cultures and backgrounds, and Shaykh Bahā'uddīn has remained closely involved in its daily life and hospitality.

Known for his warm personality, humility, accessibility, and humour, Shaykh Bahā'uddīn has become beloved among many murīds for his practical and relatable teaching style. His ṣuḥbahs often focus on sincerity, humility, detachment from ego, and preparing spiritually for the trials of modern life. A recurring theme in his teachings is the concept of fanāʾ — “being nothing” before Allāh — which he presents not as abstraction, but as a lived path of simplicity, service, and self-effacement.

In addition to preserving the legacy of his father, Shaykh Bahā'uddīn also serves as a bridge between older and younger generations of murīds, particularly among English-speaking communities connected to the Naqshbandī path. His informal and candid style has resonated strongly with many seekers navigating spirituality in the modern world, especially those drawn to an approach rooted in authenticity rather than formality.

Over the years, he has travelled internationally to participate in gatherings of dhikr, mawlids, conferences, and community events connected to the Naqshbandī-Ḥaqqānī tradition. Through these visits, he continues to strengthen ties between Naqshbandī communities across Europe, North America, Southeast Asia, Australia, Türkiye, and the Middle East.

Despite belonging to a globally recognised spiritual family, Shaykh Bahā'uddīn remains closely associated with khidmah (service), simplicity, and hospitality — qualities long regarded as hallmarks of the Naqshbandī path. Whether receiving guests in Lefke, participating in community gatherings, or delivering spiritual reminders, he is widely appreciated for embodying the intimate and familial spirit of the dergāh established by Mawlānā Shaykh Nāẓim.
