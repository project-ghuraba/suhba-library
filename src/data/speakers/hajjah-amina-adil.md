Ḥājjah Amīna ʿĀdil (Sulṭān Ḥānim) was a renowned author, lecturer, spiritual advisor, and one of the most influential women within the contemporary Naqshbandī tradition. For more than four decades, she dedicated her life to educating and guiding people from all backgrounds in understanding the teachings of Islām, with a particular emphasis on the spiritual and legal rights of women as established in the Sharīʿah.

Born in the Kazan region of Russia into a family tracing its lineage to the Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ, she grew up during the early years of Communist rule, at a time of severe religious persecution. Her early childhood was marked by displacement and hardship as her family undertook a dangerous hijrah (migration) to escape oppression, travelling under extreme conditions before eventually reaching safety in Anatolia. These formative experiences shaped her lifelong commitment to faith, resilience, justice, and service.

After settling in Turkey, and later moving to the Levant, her family eventually reached Damascus, where she entered a deeply formative spiritual environment. There she studied under prominent scholars of Islām, including Grandshaykh Shaykh ʿAbdullāh Fāʾiz ad-Dāghistānī, as well as leading jurists of her time such as Shaykh Ṣāliḥ al-Farfūr and Shaykh Mukhtār al-ʿĀlāylī. She received training in both fiqh (Islamic jurisprudence) and taṣawwuf (Islamic spirituality), developing a rare combination of scholarly depth and spiritual insight.

At the age of twenty-three, she was married—upon the guidance of Grandshaykh ʿAbdullāh—to Shaykh Muḥammad Nāẓim ʿĀdil al-Ḥaqqānī, with whom she shared nearly fifty years of companionship, travel, and service. As the wife of the 40th Grand Shaykh of the Naqshbandī order, she accompanied him across the world and became an integral part of his mission to transmit spiritual teachings globally. Together they raised a family while supporting the establishment of Naqshbandī centres across multiple continents.

Ḥājjah Amīna ʿĀdil was herself a scholar and shaykha, fluent in Turkish, Arabic, and English, and widely respected for her ability to communicate complex Islamic principles in a clear and accessible way. She delivered lectures and advised women across Europe, the Middle East, Asia, Africa, and the Americas, addressing topics related to spirituality, family life, ethics, and the unique spiritual dignity of women in Islām.

She is also the author of several major works, including the widely known *Lore of Light* series and *Muḥammad: The Messenger of Islām*, which draw upon classical sources and spiritual narrations to present the lives of the prophets and saints in an accessible form. Her writings are noted for combining scholarly rigor with spiritual reflection and storytelling.

Throughout her life, she was a central figure in hosting and receiving thousands of visitors in Cyprus, where the family later settled in Lefke. Known for her wisdom, practical counsel, and strong presence, she was frequently consulted by women and families seeking guidance in both personal and religious matters. She also accompanied Shaykh Nāẓim on selected international visits and participated in conferences addressing Islam and the role of women in contemporary society.

Ḥājjah Amīna ʿĀdil passed away on 16 November 2004, leaving behind a legacy of scholarship, spiritual teaching, and service. She is remembered within the Naqshbandī tradition as a woman of strength, knowledge, and devotion, whose life embodied patience (ṣabr), reliance upon Allāh (tawakkul), and commitment to spiritual education and family life.
