As-Salāmu ‘alaykum wa-raḥmatu Llāhi wa-barakātuh. A‘ūdhu bi-Llāhi mina sh-shayṭāni r-rajīm. Bismi Llāhi r-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm. Aṣ-ṣalātu wa-s-Salāmu ‘alá Rasūlinā Muḥammadin Sayyidi l-awwalīna wa-l-ākhirīn. Madad yā Rasūla Llāh, madad yā Sādātī Aṣḥābi Rasūli Llāh, madad yā Mashāyikhanā, dastūr Mawlānā Shaykh ‘Abdu Llāh al-Fā’iz ad-Dāghistānī, Shaykh Muḥammad Nāẓim al-Ḥaqqānī. Madad. Ṭarīqatuna ṣ-ṣuḥbah, wa-l-khayru fi l-jam’iyyah. We are in the blessed month of Muḥarram. May its virtues be upon us. Its virtues are great. Our Prophet ṣallá Llāhu ‘alayhi wa-sallam says: whoever fasts one day in Muḥarram, each day is counted as thirty days. Each day is counted as thirty days. The best practice is to fast from the first to the tenth. Those who cannot do so can fast either the ninth and tenth or the tenth and eleventh of Muḥarram. These include the day of ‘Āshūrā’, which is a great day. Allāh ‘Azza wa-Jalla has gifted it to mankind. Whoever wants may benefit from it; it is for all people. How does one benefit? By believing in Allāh and taking benefit from that day. That’s it. No one prevents people from benefitting. It is beneficial for everyone. This becomes a means. Believing in Allāh and living as He commands fills each day with virtue, reward, and beauty. Whoever does not do so misses out on a great deal. Each day is without any benefit and contains nothing but harm. Therefore, this month of Muḥarram is a blessed month. Before fasting became obligatory in Ramaḍān, they used to fast in the month of Muḥarram. After that, when it became obligatory, they began to fast in Ramaḍān. The obligation to fast was established in the month of Ramaḍān. As we said, in the month of Muḥarram, Prophets attained their spiritual ranks or were granted Prophethood. Similarly, the awliyā’ received the stations of sainthood on the day of ‘Āshūrā’, the tenth of Muḥarram. On that day, many events occurred: The forgiveness of Ādam ‘alayhi s-salām, the ship of Nūḥ ‘alayhi s-salām – the flood ended and it came to rest on the earth – the salvation of Mūsá ‘alayhi s-salām, and most notably, the ascent of Idrīs ʿalayhi s-salām to the heavens. Many events have happened on this day. Very beautiful events have taken place. That is why this is a blessed day. Our Prophet ṣallá Llāhu ‘alayhi wa-sallam used to fast on it. When he arrived in Madīnah and saw that the Jews were fasting on that day, he said, “I am more deserving of this day.” So, in opposition to them, he fasted either the ninth and tenth or the tenth and eleventh. As we mentioned, this blessed day carries great spiritual benefit and also material benefit. On that day, the Prophet ṣallā Llāhu ‘alayhi wa-sallam said: whoever showers will enjoy good health throughout the year. On that day, whoever applies kohl to their eyes will not suffer eye pain. Whoever gives ṣadaqah will be granted abundance. Whoever provides for their family – whether by buying gifts for their children or giving them things they ask for – that year will be filled with barakah for them, with the permission of Allāh. Alongside the spiritual, there are also outward blessings. There is certainly benefit in every command of Islām, and harm in every prohibition. A person’s nafs (ego) desires to do ḥarām things. That only makes the nafs worse, and also harms the body. That is why whatever Allāh ‘Azza wa-Jalla has gifted in Islām is certainly beneficial and beautiful – both outward and inward. Today, of course, the blessed grandson of our Prophet ṣallā Llāhu ‘alayhi wa-sallam was also martyred along with ṣaḥābah. Their martyrdom is a great blessing for every Muslim in Islām. He is Sayyidu l-Shuhadā’. He is the greatest of martyrs, the master. That’s why he attained that rank. With that rank, he is among the highest ranks before Allāh, beside his grandfather. Sadness occurs when there is no rank, when there is nothing. Our Prophet ṣallá Llāhu ‘alayhi wa-sallam says, “The pain that a martyr feels is not even like the prick of a needle.” So, the apple of his blessed eye, his beloved grandson, when he attained martyrdom, felt pleasure in the most beautiful states. As he was being martyred, he felt that beautiful joy – Ḥusayn ‘alayhi s-salām, the grandson of our Prophet. Therefore, some of these things that are not in accordance with the path shown by our Prophet and Islām are not acceptable. Whoever resorts to them acts in vain and gains no benefit at all. Allāh ‘Azza wa-Jalla has elevated him to the most beautiful ranks. May Allāh settle us near him in paradise, in shā’a Llāh. That is it, we say. May they have lofty maqāms. And may their blessings be upon us. That martyrdom is a very great rank. May that martyrdom intercede for us, in shā’a Llāh. May Allāh make this day of ours blessed. Today is not the tenth – today is the ninth. Tomorrow is the tenth. We are saying this now to carry out those duties tomorrow. There is also a four-raka‘āt prayer tomorrow. It may be performed at any time, but it must be before ‘Aṣr. After ‘Aṣr, nafl prayers are not allowed. That prayer is four raka‘āt, and in each rak‘ah, you will recite Sūratu l-Ikhlāṣ eleven times. After completing the prayer, you will recite seventy times: “Ḥasbuna Llāhu wa-ni‘ma l-wakīl, ni‘ma l-mawlá wa-ni‘ma n-naṣīr, ghufrānaka rabbanā wa-ilayka l-maṣīr.” Then there is a du‘ā’. That du‘ā’ will be recited seven times, in shā’a Llāh. Through that du‘ā’, the barakah of the day will reach us, in shā’a Llāh. May Allāh make it blessed. May their barakah be upon us. May those maqāms also benefit us, in shā’a Llāh. May the maqāms of the prophets, the awliyā’, the righteous, the martyrs, and the most distinguished martyrs be exalted, in shā’a Llāh.
