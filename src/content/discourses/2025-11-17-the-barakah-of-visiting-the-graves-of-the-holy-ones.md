As-Salāmu ‘alaykum wa-raḥmatu Llāhi wa-barakātuh. A‘ūdhu bi-Llāhi mina sh-shayṭāni r-rajīm. Bismi Llāhi r-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm. Aṣ-ṣalātu wa-s-Salāmu ‘alá Rasūlinā Muḥammadin Sayyidi l-awwalīna wa-l-ākhirīn. Madad yā Rasūla Llāh, A‘ūdhu bi-Llāhi mina sh-shayṭāni r-rajīm. Bismi Llāhi r-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm. madad yā Sādātī Aṣḥābi, madad yā Mashāyikhanā, Mawlānā Shaykh ‘Abdu Llāh al-Fā’iz ad-Dāghistānī, Shaykh Muḥammad Nāẓim al-Ḥaqqānī. Madad. Ṭarīqatuna ṣ-ṣuḥbah, wa-l-khayru fi l-jam’iyyah. Our Prophet ṣallá Llāhu ‘alayhi wa-sallam praised this island, the island we are sitting on. Shukr to Allāh, this is a place honoured by Muslims from the very beginning of Islām. These places are the places chosen by Allāh ‘Azza wa-Jalla. Both Islām and all the prophets – or rather, most of the prophets – came from this region: the Ḥijāz, Shām, Yemen – from these parts. Of course, Allāh sent prophets to every part of the world; He sent prophets to everyone. But because most of the prophets came from these places, these are blessed places. They are blessed because they are places where prophets travelled and conveyed the message. This is the place of Islām and of humanity – the place where they were born. Allāh ‘Azza wa-Jalla created Ādam ‘alayhi s-salām in Paradise, and he remained there. When he was sent down to Earth, most of the prophets from his lineage, from his descendants, lived on these lands. Their blessed places, their blessed noble graves are many in these parts. Visiting them brings both barakah and the himmah of those Prophets to the one who visits. Then, visiting the companions, the Ahlu l-Bayt, visiting all of them, visiting the graves of the awliyā’, and the righteous, brings to a person, to a Muslim, this barakah, the descending manifestation, this mercy, because in the place where they are, that mercy descends there until Qiyāmah. Therefore, visiting them also benefits the one who visits. Don’t listen to those foolish people today. They say, “You worship graves.” No, why should we worship graves? If we are going to worship, we know where we will worship. Not according to what you say. We worship Allāh ‘Azza wa-Jalla. We worship facing that qiblah. We are going on the way shown by our Prophet ṣallá Llāhu ‘alayhi wa-sallam, shukr to Allāh. Here on our island, there are blessed Companions and Awliyā’. Some of them are known, some remain unknown. Even Barnabas, one of the disciples of ‘Īsá ‘alayhi s-salām who wrote the true gospel, is here. His noble grave is here. He is visited as well. That has nothing to do with Christianity. He is not one who worships idols or pieces of wood. His gospel is the true gospel. They don’t show it – that is another matter. But he was present in the journeys of Prophet ‘Īsá ‘alayhi s-salām. He was present in his journeys, he was with him. It is the true gospel that he wrote. It explains and indicates that Prophet ‘Īsá was a prophet. Even in well-known sources, even the Christians know him as Barnabas. But they only know the name. They know nothing else. They do not know that a gospel was written. That gospel exists, but of course if that gospel came out all these false things would be exposed at once. They would be left without a religion. Out of necessity they would all have to become Muslims. They do not want that either. For worldly benefit, for gain, for power, for all sorts of things they hide it, they conceal it – only so that shayṭān’s command may be obeyed, so that they may be with shayṭān. That is why these matters are important. Graves are not for worship. They are visited to receive their barakah and the mercy that descends upon them. And also for barakah, and also so that we may see how beautifully these people lived, how they served Islām, how they obeyed Allāh, and how they showed people the way. That is something of immense importance and immense beauty. Visiting the graves of the righteous and of the prophets. First and foremost – the crown of our heads, the light of our eyes – is to visit the noble grave of our Prophet ṣallá Llāhu ‘alayhi wa-sallam. After that, the companions, the Ahlu l-Bayt and the rest are all visited; their barakah is obtained by Allāh’s permission. There are many people who confuse people’s minds. Do not listen to them. They neither know what they say nor understand what they read, nor will they abandon their stubbornness. They are unimportant. What is important is that Allāh has created us in these beautiful lands and has placed us on this beautiful path. May Allāh make this path everlasting. May we remain on it forever, in shā’a Llāh.
