As-salāmu ʿalaykum wa raḥmatullāhi wa barakātuh.

Aʿūdhu billāhi min ash-shayṭāni r-rajīm. Bismillāhi r-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm. Aṣ-ṣalātu wa s-salāmu ʿalā Rasūlinā Muḥammadin Sayyidi l-awwalīna wa l-ākhirīn. Madad yā RasūlAllāh, madad yā Sādāti Aṣḥābi Rasūlillāh, madad yā Mashāyikhinā, dastūr yā Shaykh ʿAbdullāh al-Fā’iz ad-Dāghistānī, Shaykh Muḥammad Nāẓim al-Ḥaqqānī. Madad. Ṭarīqatunā aṣ-ṣuḥbah wa l-khayru fi l-jamʿiyyah.

Bismillāhi r-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm,<br>
أَلَآ إِنَّ أَوْلِيَآءَ ٱللَّهِ لَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَكَانُوا۟ يَتَّقُونَ
<br>“Alā inna awliyā'Allahi lā khawfun ʿalayhim wa lā hum yaḥzanūn. Alladhīna āmanū wa kānū yattaqūn.”<br>
“Verily on the friends of Allah there is no fear, nor shall they grieve. Those who believed and have been God-fearing.” (Qurʼān 10:62-63). ṢadaqAllāhu l-ʿAẓīm.

Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla says in Qur’ān ʿAẓīmu sh-Sha’n, the servants whom Allah loves are the Awliyā. For them, there is neither fear, nor sorrow, nor sadness—because they fear only Allah. Nothing else concerns them or weighs upon their hearts. Since everything occurs by the command of Allah, they live their lives in accordance with His command—following His path and the path of the Prophets—while being of benefit to others.

Whereas in this world—which is not Paradise—there will inevitably be challenges; discomforts and trials are a natural part of life. These trials vary from person to person—some may face more, others less. Awliyā’ullāh recognize that these trials come from Allah, and thus, they are not troubled by them. They accept this as the destiny ordained for them by Allah, and they live accordingly.

Throughout our lives, we remain on the path of Allah. How does one follow Allah’s path? By performing one’s prayers and fulfilling all the fard—everything that Allah has commanded—to the best of one’s ability, and by remaining steadfast in these acts. The more good deeds and acts of worship you perform, the closer you draw to Allah.

A Ḥadīth Qudsī states,<br>
يَتَقَرَّبُ العَبْد إلَيَّ بالنَّوافِلِ
<br>“Yataqarrabu al-ʿabdu ilayya bi-l-nawāfil.”<br>
“The believer draws ever closer to Me through voluntary acts of worship, nawāfil.” [Bukhārī]

Being close to Allah is the greatest benefit a human being can attain; conversely, being distant from Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla is a truly dire state. Because when a person drifts away from Allah, they mistakenly attribute their troubles to themselves—believing they are of their own making or the result of their own actions.

Yet, as we have mentioned, this life is a place of testing, and these trials are ordained by Allah. Those who are distant from Allah remain unaware of this reality; having no knowledge of the tests, Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla, the Prophet, the religion or faith. They live in a state of constant distress. They imagine that they control everything and that all outcomes originate from themselves.

In truth, if they were to submit to the decree of Allah, such sorrow would not linger, for they would know that the ultimate outcome will be good.

Conversely, if one ignores His decree, acts according to one’s own whims, distances oneself from Allah, and commits various wrongs in an attempt to satisfy one’s own desires, the situation only worsens.

Therefore, one must strive to be among the beloved servants of Allah and remain upon the path that He desires. If you hold yourself accountable “Am I good or not?” And are consistent in performing the acts of worship—then you are an accepted servant. If, however, you abandon them or fail to perform them, know that you have erred; turn back from that error and begin again.

No human is free from fault; everyone makes mistakes. But, shukr to Allah, He is the Forgiver—He who grants pardon and always accepts repentance. When you turn back to Allah, He transforms your past mistakes into rewards and elevates your station. May Allah protect us all and count us among His beloved servants, inshā’Allāh.

Wa min Allahi t-Tawfīq.

Al-Fātiḥa.
