Auzu Billahi Minashaytanir Rajeem. Bismillahir Rahmanir Raheem,

Wassalatu Wassalamu ala Rasulina Muhammadin Sayyidul Awwalin wal Akhirin,

Madad Ya Rasulallah, Madad Ya As’habi Rasulillah, Madad Ya Mashayikhina,

Sheykh Abdullah Daghestani, Sheykh Nazim al-Haqqani. Dastur.

Humans need to act like Sons of Adam. We need to be like our father Adam and not like Shaitan. Adam (Alayhis Salam) said to Allah (Azza wa Jalla), “We have committed sin.” He pleaded, “We have oppressed ourselves. It is our fault. Forgive us.” Shaitan said to Allah (Azza wa Jalla), “You mislead me from the way!” He talked nonsense saying he would do this and that. He made statements close to a threat. Who do you think you are?

The person that does not know boundaries is like Shaitan. The person who is a human being and Son of Adam is like our father Adam such that they admit their fault. Allah (Azza wa Jalla) created humans not so they may make mistakes, but so they may beat their ego and Shaitan and become good people. Most humans are like Shaitan. They do not admit their own faults and shortcomings, immediately flare up, and try to show themselves as blameless. They make up hundreds of lies and do thousands of tricks trying to show themselves on the right. They constantly make themselves right.

Actually, the person who is right does not fall into bad situations. But the perwon who is wrong continuously tries all ways to show themselves good and not to admit their mistakes. It is a good thing to apologize when you do something wrong and commit a sin. People would accept you more and forgive you then. Otherwise, they would not believe you and they would no longer have regard for you.

These behaviors have existed ever since the times Adam (Alayhis Salam) arrived. Allah (Azza wa Jalla) is showing the side that is good so that you do this and become an acceptable person. If not, you look like Shaitan and are not held in esteem. People would hate you and become your enemies. You would be left alone. May Allah protect us.

Wa Minallah at-Tawfeeq.

Al-Fatiha.
