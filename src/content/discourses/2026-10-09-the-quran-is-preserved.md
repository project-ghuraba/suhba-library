As-salāmu ʿalaykum wa raḥmatullāhi wa barakātuh.

Aʿūdhu billāhi min ash-shayṭāni r-rajīm. Bismillāhi r-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm. Aṣ-ṣalātu wa s-salāmu ʿalā Rasūlinā Muḥammadin Sayyidi l-awwalīna wa l-ākhirīn. Madad yā RasūlAllāh, madad yā Sādāti Aṣḥābi Rasūlillāh, madad yā Mashāyikhinā, dastūr yā Shaykh ʿAbdullāh al-Fā’iz ad-Dāghistānī, Shaykh Muḥammad Nāẓim al-Ḥaqqānī. Madad. Ṭarīqatunā aṣ-ṣuḥbah wa l-khayru fi l-jamʿiyyah.

Bismillāhi r-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm,<br>
إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَزَّلْنَا ٱلذِّكْرَ وَإِنَّا لَهُۥ لَحَٰفِظُونَ
<br>“Innā naḥnu nazzalnā dh-dhikra wa-innā lahu laḥāfiẓūn”<br>
“Indeed, it is We who sent down the Qur’an and indeed, We will be its guardian.” (Qurʼān 15:9). ṢadaqAllāhu l-ʿAẓīm.

Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla says: We revealed Qur’ān ʿAẓīmu sh-Sha’n to the Prophet ṣallAllāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam, and We preserve it. Nothing of it is lost or altered. For Qur’ān ʿAẓīmu sh-Sha’n is the Word of Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla, His eternal Word; thus, it remains under divine protection.

Of course, scriptures were revealed to many other prophets as well. Yet, none of them have remained intact. Indeed, hardly a fraction of the original text survives—if any at all. Through repeated alteration, they were eventually molded into whatever form people desired. They are called “holy books,” yet their sanctity has been lost, for people concealed some parts and voiced others according to their own minds.

Qur’ān ʿAẓīmu sh-Sha’n, however, stands among humanity as the very Word and Book of Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla; therefore, nothing within it can be altered.

Sometimes, when reciting or speaking, we may make mistakes, and others may recite it incorrectly too. That is not an issue; it causes no harm, for it is under divine protection.

However, it is not acceptable if a mistake completely distorts the meaning. Of course, Allah forgives. If a person speaks incorrectly out of ignorance or by mistake—for no one would do so intentionally— Allah forgives them.

There are non-Arabs. Arabs—since Arabic is the language of the Qurʼān—are a bit better at reciting it without error. As for non-Arabs—referred to as Ajam—their tongues may struggle with certain letters or sounds; they may pronounce some correctly while mispronouncing others. Our Prophet ṣallAllāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam states, “There are two angels; when a person recites the Qurʼān, they correct it and ascend with it, sending it up to the presence of Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla.” Therefore, there is no harm or issue in this. One should simply do as much as one is able.

Humans are prone to errors and shortcomings; Allah forgives. As long as the intention is sincere, it is all accepted. So, one should not worry by saying, “I cannot recite it perfectly.” No matter how one recites it—as we said—there are angels who correct it, elevate it in a more beautiful form to the presence of Allah, and Allah accepts it.

Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla loves those who honor the Qur’ān and worship Him with reverence for it. May Allah make us all among His beloved servants, inshā’Allāh. May Allah be pleased with you.

We, too, are very fortunate people. We are fortunate to be part of the Ummah of our Prophet ṣallAllāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam and to live in this time; to read, memorize, and—to the best of our ability— put into practice that Book, the Word of Allah.

This is the greatest fortune; poverty or illness—none of that truly matters. What is important is to be on the way of Allah—to walk on the way of Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla and recite His beautiful Word. That Word is healing, it is light, and it is goodness of every kind. May Allah protect us, increase it in our hearts, and not let us forget it, inshā’Allāh.

Wa min Allahi t-Tawfīq.

Al-Fātiḥa.

There are recited Qur’ān Khatms Shukr to Allah. There are hundreds of Qur’ān Khatms, tasbihat, tahlilāt, salawat, charity and khatms of Dalā’il Al-Khayrāt.

We gift them to the soul of our Prophet ṣallAllāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam, for the souls of his Ahlu l-Bayt and ṣaḥābah. For the souls of all prophets, Awliyā’, asfiya, mashāyikh.

To the souls of all our ancestors.

To the souls of all Muslimin and Muslimat, Mu’minin and Mu’minat.

For goodness to come and evil to be warded off. May whatever good intentions those who recite it have be attained.

For happiness here and hereafter, inshā’Allāh.

Li-Llahi Ta’ala, Al-Fātiḥah.
