As-salāmu ʿalaykum wa raḥmatullāhi wa barakātuh.

Aʿūdhu billāhi min ash-shayṭāni r-rajīm. Bismillāhi r-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm. Aṣ-ṣalātu wa s-salāmu ʿalā Rasūlinā Muḥammadin Sayyidi l-awwalīna wa l-ākhirīn. Madad yā RasūlAllāh, madad yā Sādāti Aṣḥābi Rasūlillāh, madad yā Mashāyikhinā, dastūr yā Shaykh ʿAbdullāh al-Fā’iz ad-Dāghistānī, Shaykh Muḥammad Nāẓim al-Ḥaqqānī. Madad. Ṭarīqatunā aṣ-ṣuḥbah wa l-khayru fi l-jamʿiyyah.

Bismillāhi r-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm,<br>
إِنَّا جَعَلْنَا مَا عَلَى الْأَرْضِ زِينَةً لَّهَا لِنَبْلُوَهُمْ أَيُّهُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا (7) وَإِنَّا لَجَاعِلُونَ مَا عَلَيْهَا صَعِيدًا جُرُزًا
<br>“Innā jaʿalnā mā ʿalā l-arḍi zīnatan lahā li-nabluwahum ayyuhum aḥsanu ʿamalā. Wa innā la-jāʿilūna mā ʿalayhā ṣaʿīdan juruzā.”<br>
“Verily! We have made that which is on earth as an adornment for it, in order that We may test them (mankind) as to which of them are best in deeds. And verily! We shall make all that is on it (the earth) a bare dry soil.” (Qurʼān 18:7-8). ṢadaqAllāhu l-ʿAẓīm.

In the āyah we recited in the Khutbah, Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla states that He adorned this world. Everything is beautiful; indeed, everything created by Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla is beautiful. He created all things with perfect order and precise calculation. He fashioned the world in the most beautiful way, yet mankind has ruined it by doing things that Allah does not approve of.

Consequently, the world has lost its splendor. Furthermore, Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla declares that when qiyāmah draws near, He will reduce everything to a barren, level plain—“صَعِيدًا جُرُزًا” “Ṣaʿīdan juruzā.” “Ṣaʿīdan juruzā,” means there will be no mountains, no hills, no valleys—nothing at all. There will be no grass, no vegetation, and no trees; He will render it completely dry and desolate.

By stating this—that nothing will remain in this world—Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla is telling mankind not to attach value to this world. Not only will everything end when you depart from it, but in the end, the world itself will cease to exist; all its adornments and trappings will disappear entirely. People, during the few years they live, often imagine that this world will never end and that nothing will ever change. But Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla affirms that both this world and humanity itself will come to an end. Thereafter, they will attain the true, eternal life.

In this regard, there is the blessed saying of our Prophet ṣallAllāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam,<br>
الدُّنيا سِجنُ المُؤمِنِ، وجَنَّةُ الكافِرِ 
<br>“Ad-dunyā sijnu l-mu'min wa-jannatu l-kāfir”<br>
“The world is the prison of the believer and the paradise of the disbeliever.” [Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim]

It is said that for the believer, the world is a place of imprisonment—meaning that, when compared to the Hereafter, even the most abundant worldly life is akin to being in a prison.

For the disbeliever, however, it is a paradise; they have been deceived by the world and have become completely immersed in it, believing that everything belongs to them. They boast, “I have done this and that; I am so powerful; I possess so much—wealth, property, and men (followers).” From their perspective, this is a paradise.

Even if a disbeliever were to find themselves in the worst possible state in this life—even if they were to die of hunger—it would still be a “paradise” for them when compared to the hardships of the Hereafter. This is because the torment of the Hereafter is immense for disbelievers, those who do not possess faith; thus, the world is considered a paradise for them.

Once, Shaykh Abdul Qādir Al-Gaylāni was traveling with his disciples, mounted on a horse. An elderly non-Muslim man approached, took hold of the horse’s bridle, and asked, “How can you say that this world is like a prison for you, yet a paradise for us?” Shaykh Abdul Qādir Al-Gaylāni replied, “When viewed in light of the Hereafter, all these worldly things amount to nothing—hence, it is like a prison. For you, however, your current situation is considered a paradise when compared to what awaits in the Hereafter.”

Upon hearing this, the man embraced the faith and attained salvation. Whoever comes to faith finds salvation; as for those who do not, the choice—and the consequence—is their own.

Therefore, it is not wise to attach great importance to the fleeting goods of this world. It is not good to be deceived by the world. It is not good to chase after this world. Allah grants what He wills; as for what He does not grant—Allah knows best.

Whatever is destined—one’s kismet (allotted share)—will come to pass. May Allah grant us the kismet, that which is good and blessed. May we not be deluded by the world, inshā’Allāh.

Wa min Allahi t-Tawfīq.

Al-Fātiḥa.
