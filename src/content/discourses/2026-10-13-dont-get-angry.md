As-salāmu ʿalaykum wa raḥmatullāhi wa barakātuh.

Aʿūdhu billāhi min ash-shayṭāni r-rajīm. Bismillāhi r-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm. Aṣ-ṣalātu wa s-salāmu ʿalā Rasūlinā Muḥammadin Sayyidi l-awwalīna wa l-ākhirīn. Madad yā RasūlAllāh, madad yā Sādāti Aṣḥābi Rasūlillāh, madad yā Mashāyikhinā, dastūr yā Shaykh ʿAbdullāh al-Fā’iz ad-Dāghistānī, Shaykh Muḥammad Nāẓim al-Ḥaqqānī. Madad. Ṭarīqatunā aṣ-ṣuḥbah wa l-khayru fi l-jamʿiyyah.

A Companion once asked our Prophet ṣallAllāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam, “Give me a piece of advice—tell me something that I may hold fast to.” Our Prophet ṣallAllāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam replied, “لا تغضب”, “Lā taghdab”—meaning, “Do not get angry.” 

He asked a second time, and received the same answer, “Do not get angry.” Regarding the third time, the Companion remarked, “Had I continued asking until morning, our Prophet ṣallAllāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam would have said the very same thing.”

This is a matter of great importance. “Do not get angry.” One might ask, “But how do I avoid getting angry? How do we learn this?” Our Prophet ṣallAllāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam explained it thus,<br>
إِنَّمَا الْعِلْمُ بِالتَّعَلُّمِ، وَإِنَّمَا الْحِلْمُ بِالتَّحَلُّمِ
<br>“Innamā l-ʿilmu bi-t-taʿallumi, wa-innamā al-ḥilmu bi-t-taḥallumi”<br>
“Knowledge is only by learning, and patience is only by exerting oneself to be patient.”

Knowledge comes through learning; gradually, day by day. One cannot acquire all knowledge in a single day. Likewise, ḥilm—that is, the quality of not giving in to anger—is learned gradually.

As the Turkish saying goes, “It does not happen instantly—like dipping something into a dye vat and pulling it out changed,” rather, it comes about little by little.

There is no need to blow things out of proportion whenever you encounter the trivial matters that might provoke anger. Remind yourself, “I should not get angry at this. It is unnecessary, pointless, and uncalled for. What good would come of getting angry? I could make that same mistake, too. So, there is no need for anger.” When it happens again, the same thought should cross your mind again, saying, “Well, they made a mistake; no one is infallible,” and once more, you do not get angry. After doing this a few times, you train yourself not to get angry over trivial matters.

Of course, there are things worth getting angry about—specifically, actions committed against Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla. There is a time and place for anger, and there are times when it is inappropriate. If you get angry without cause, you will spend your entire life in a state of anger and unrest. Conversely, if you restrain your anger, you remain calm and at ease; you find peace.

As the saying goes, “Sharp vinegar damages its own jar.” When vinegar is too sharp, it corrodes the very vessel holding it. Anger works the same way; if you give in to anger, you suffer more harm than the person you are angry with. May Allah grant us all forbearance and knowledge, inshā’Allāh.

May we walk the way of Allah, inshā’Allāh.

Wa min Allahi t-Tawfīq.

Al-Fātiḥa.
