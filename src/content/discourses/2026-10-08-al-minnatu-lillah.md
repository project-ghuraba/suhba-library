As-salāmu ʿalaykum wa raḥmatullāhi wa barakātuh.

Aʿūdhu billāhi min ash-shayṭāni r-rajīm. Bismillāhi r-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm. Aṣ-ṣalātu wa s-salāmu ʿalā Rasūlinā Muḥammadin Sayyidi l-awwalīna wa l-ākhirīn. Madad yā RasūlAllāh, madad yā Sādāti Aṣḥābi Rasūlillāh, madad yā Mashāyikhinā, dastūr yā Shaykh ʿAbdullāh al-Fā’iz ad-Dāghistānī, Shaykh Muḥammad Nāẓim al-Ḥaqqānī. Madad. Ṭarīqatunā aṣ-ṣuḥbah wa l-khayru fi l-jamʿiyyah.

Bismillāhi r-Raḥmāni r-Raḥīm,<br>
لَ‍قَ‍‍‍دْ‌ مَ‍‍نَّ ‌اللَّ‍‍هُ عَلَى‌ ‌الْمُؤْمِن‍‍ِ‍ي‍‍نَ ‌إِ‌ذْ‌ بَعَثَ فِيهِمْ ‌‍رَسُولا‌ ً‌ مِ‍‌‍نْ ‌أَ‌نْ‍‍فُسِهِمْ يَتْلُو‌ا‌ عَلَيْهِمْ ‌آيَاتِ‍‍هِ ‌وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ ‌وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ ‌الْكِت‍‍َ‍ابَ ‌وَ‌الْحِكْمَةَ ‌وَ‌إِ‌نْ كَانُو‌ا‌ مِ‍‌‍نْ قَ‍‍‍بْ‍‍لُ لَفِي ضَ‍‍لاَلٍ‌ مُبِينٍ
<br>“Laqad mannAllāh ʿala l-mu'minīn idh baʿatha fīhim rasūlan min anfusihim yatlū ʿalayhim āyātihī wa yuzakkīhim wa yuʿallimuhumu l-kitāb wa l-ḥikmah wa in kānū min qabl lafī ḍalālin mubīn.”<br>
“Indeed Allah conferred a great favour on the believers when He sent among them a Messenger (Muḥammad ṣallAllāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam) from among themselves, reciting unto them His Verses (the Qur’ān), and purifying them, and instructing them (in) the Book (the Qur’ān) and al-­Hikmah [the Wisdom], while before that they had been in manifest error.” (Qurʼān 3:164). ṢadaqAllāhu l-ʿAẓīm.

The prerogative of conferring favor belongs to Allah. What does this mean? It refers to the act of declaring a good deed one has performed. It is not fitting for anyone other than Allah to claim such a prerogative—that is, for a person to do something and then say, “I did this; I bestowed a favor upon you.” This prerogative belongs solely to Allah; it is His right alone to perform a good deed and then make it known.

The greatest favor was bestowed upon the people of Mecca—those whom this ayah karīmah addresses as ummi (unlettered), meaning people who could neither read nor write. Most people in that era were like that; they were unlearned and knew nothing. Allah sent our Prophet ṣallAllāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam and elevated them to the highest rank among mankind.

Thus, it is Allah—who confers this favor upon them. Someone else—such as our Prophet ṣallAllāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam—could have claimed such a prerogative, yet out of his humility, he never did so.

No one possesses majesty and the prerogative of conferring favor as He does; this belongs solely to Allah. Allah possesses certain attributes that do not belong to human beings; it is not appropriate for a human to act in such a manner.

Therefore, whenever Allah guides someone onto the way of Islām and faith, the favor is from Allah. This state is expressed by the phrase “Al-Minnatu lillāh.”

Mawlānā Shaykh Nāẓim’s grandfather was a calligrapher; we possess a plaque of his work on which he inscribed “Al-Minnatu lillāh.” The prerogative of conferring favor belongs to Allah. It is not a virtuous trait for anyone else to hold a good deed over another’s head—saying, “I did this for you, I did that for you”—or to claim such a prerogative.

That is why it is said, “Do not hold the good deeds you have done over people’s heads.” A person who exacts gratitude performs a good deed and then throws it in the recipient’s face, saying, “I did this; I did that favor for you.” This is not a nice thing. If you have done a good deed, let Allah alone know it; do it for the sake of Allah. If you demand gratitude, you lose its reward—or rather, instead of receiving a hundredfold reward, you receive only one.

But if you act the other way, Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla records all those rewards for you; your station is exalted, and your spiritual strength increases further.

Therefore, a good deed done for others is actually a grace and a gift from Allah to you. Through your way and by your means, good has reached others, or guidance has been conveyed—whatever the good may be, you have served as the means; thus, both you and the other party have gained. So, do not lose this beautiful state—let us not lose it, inshā’Allāh.

The human ego desires to boast, to rise above, and to appear superior to others. These are ailments of the ego. As we have said, there are certain matters requiring attention. When speaking or acting, one must be mindful and choose the best course of action.

Indeed, just as there are attributes that Allah ʿAzza wa Jalla loves, there are also attributes He dislikes. Let us be mindful of these. May Allah not let any of us stray from His way; may He grant us vigilance, so that we may be aware of what we say, inshā’Allāh.

Wa min Allahi t-Tawfīq.

Al-Fātiḥa.
