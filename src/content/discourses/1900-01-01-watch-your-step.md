There is peacefulness but shayṭān doesn't want anywhere to be peaceful. It always wants chaos, unrest and its desire is to burn people with unrest. It can't bear seeing a peaceful place, a peaceful person; can't bear seeing a peaceful country. It immediately intends to do something that will disturb them. It has already tied heedless people to itself. Heedless people are tied to shayṭān, and have become its slaves. Heedless people don't know who they work for, where they step. That's why it isn't permissible for a believer, for believers to be heedless. If a person isn't aware who he is serving - if his deeds are satanic or godly - it isn't possible for him to escape from shayṭān's traps. He will be a slave to shayṭān. If he doesn't know the value of his deeds, for whom he is doing them, he is heedless. Being heedless is the biggest trouble for human beings, especially for the believers.

‘Abdu l-Khāliq al-Ghujduwānī Hz., may Allāh sanctify his secret - our shaykh Shaykh, ‘Abdu-Llāh ad-Dāghistānī, he is Sulṭānu l-Awliyā’, related this story - Ghujduwānī Hz. said: "For 37 years I haven't even stepped in dirt, nor did I jump over it." Naẓar bar qadam - an-Naqshbandī's first principle is naẓar bar qadam - Keeping en eye on where you step. So keeping an eye where he is stepping physically, and also in reality being aware of where and for whom he is stepping - this is the meaning of naẓar bar qadam. Keep your eye on your feet. So for whom, to whom do you step? Know this. "I haven't stepped in dirt or jumped over it for 37 years" he says. If he is aware and careful of course Allāh Almighty (jj) protects him for the rest of his life. No doubt about it. So not to be heedless is a sign especially for the Naqshbandīs.

An-Naqshbandī is the ṭarīqatu l-‘aliyya. This ṭarīqatu l-‘aliyya is the founder of 41 ṭarīqahs. The others are to train the ordinary people. Naqshbandī ṭarīqatu l-‘aliyya, is for sanding of the scholars, who are proud to be educated. It is to cut their wild attributes, and abrade them. That's the reason for the Khatm-i Khājagān. Khājagān is a sign that it is the ṭarīqah of scholars, well educated ones, hodjas. Because the fitnah of an educated person is different. Ordinary people have no claim but the scholars have claims. They claim knowledge, they claim they know. What do they know? What do we know?

‘Abdu l-Wahhāb Sha‘rānī, may Allāh sanctify his secret, says that: "Allāh graced me. I am in Allāh's grace. Beyond what He gave to common people, He gave me more, graced me." About His favours, there is a book called kitābu l-minan (Letâif-ül-minen) this book has thousands of pages, if it is written with these new letters and translated. If you try to explain it, maybe ten thousand pages aren't enough. It is a perfect work, may Allāh sanctify his secret, ‘Abdu l-Wahhāb Sha‘rānī Hz. In his book this saint declares, he says that on the Day of Judgement He (jj) will call a scholar to His presence. He judges him and asks him: "Why did you claim that you were a scholar?" He will say: "Because I memorized the glorious Qur’ān. I had thousands of verses in my memory. I could read those verses anytime because they were in my memory." "That is my word, it belongs to Me" says Allāh. "What else were you claiming as a scholar?" He will say "I memorized all the ḥadīth. I could say and explain the ḥadīth whenever necessary." "That one belongs to My beloved" says Allāh (jj).
